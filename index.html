About

From Moscow to the Frontier of Construction

I hold a master's degree in Robotics and Complex Automation from Bauman Moscow State Technical University. I started my first company in my second year — backed by investors who saw potential in my drive. That began a journey across Russia, Singapore, Germany, and the United States, founding six companies with two successful exits.

At Mighty Buildings, I took a materials science hypothesis — that photopolymer composites could replace steel rebar in construction — from unproven concept to the first UL-listed 3D-printed building elements in the world. We got there in under a year, co-authored UL3401 and ISO/ASTM 52939, and reached $20M in revenue and 60+ buildings delivered.

Through BuildTech Ventures, I now apply that pattern across a broader portfolio: spatial computing, carbon tech, AI workflows, logistics intelligence, compliance automation. I work with early-stage teams on the evaluation and de-risking phase — the part that determines whether a moonshot-level idea is worth pursuing at all.

Principles

How I Think

Principles forged through 15+ years of building companies across domains that didn't exist yet.

01

Move fast on hard problems

Physics, chemistry, manufacturing, and regulation simultaneously — that's where the real leverage is. The intersection of disciplines is where breakthroughs happen.

02

Find customers before you have a product

Understand workflows and pain points first. Make sure your value proposition sits at an intersection customers actually recognize and care about.

03

Provide 80% improvement in a digestible slice

Don't try to change everything at once. Deliver transformative improvement within a workflow people already understand.

04

Standards are competitive advantages

Co-authoring UL3401 and ISO/ASTM 52939 wasn't compliance work — it was building the moat. Those who shape the rules shape the market.

05

Build the proof, then the company

A technically ambitious idea with high uncertainty, a regulatory barrier, and a small team trying to figure out if it can actually work — that's where I thrive.

06

Treat matter as meaning

Every material decision carries cultural significance and emotional depth. Technology should deepen — not replace — our connection to materiality.
